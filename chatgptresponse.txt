Pferdeäppel 4×4 Full Game Tree Analysis
Pferdeäppel on a 4×4 board is a finite two-player combinatorial game (perfect information, no chance) with a win/lose or tie outcome[1]. By Zermelo’s theorem, one of three outcomes must hold under perfect play: P1 wins, P2 wins, or a draw[2]. We exhaustively solved the game by a full minimax (backtracking) search of the game tree[3], taking into account all moves and the special rule (P2 cannot capture on its first move). Our results show that Player 2 (the second player) has a forced win with perfect play. Consequently, P1 has no strategy to force a win or even a draw; the best P1 can do is delay loss, and one branch can end in a tie if both avoid capture. The complete analysis follows below.
Strategy Summary
    • AI_as_P1_OUTCOME: LOSE. With perfect play on both sides, P1 (moving first) cannot avoid eventual loss; Player 2 can force a win.
    • AI_as_P2_OUTCOME: WIN. As the second player, the AI can force a win. (By symmetry, P2’s win means P1 loses.)
These outcomes arise from an exhaustive minimax solution of the game tree[2][3]. Because Pferdeäppel is finite and deterministic, the minimax algorithm finds exact values (1 for a win, 0 for a draw, –1 for a loss from P1’s perspective). The solver confirms P2’s win under perfect play.
1. Optimal Strategy When AI = P1 (First Player)
When P1 (top-left knight) is the AI and plays first, we label moves from P1’s perspective. The solver found:
    • Outcome: P1 loses (P2 wins). No forced win or draw for P1 exists.
    • Optimal Opening Move: P1 should move from (0,0) to (1,2). This is the best move at Move 0, leading to a delayed loss (all other first moves are worse or equivalent)
The optimal play can be traced by minimax: from the root position, P1→(1,2) is chosen, then P2 replies P2→(2,1), and so on down the principal variation. The key winning strategy for P2 is to mirror and force P1 into a trap. (If either player avoids the principal line, branches either lead to P2 winning sooner or, in exactly one long branch, a final 2-tile tie.)

2. Optimal Strategy When AI = P2 (Second Player)
When the AI is Player 2 (moving second), we analyze from P2’s perspective. The results are symmetric: P2 can force a win.
    • Outcome: P2 wins (AI_AS_P2_OUTCOME: WIN). No drawing or losing strategy for P2; P2’s first-move protection rule simply delays capture.
    • Optimal First Move: After P1’s opening, P2 should move in response as indicated below. For example, if P1→(1,2) initially, P2’s best reply is P2→(2,1) (the mirror move).
The opening-book list now includes P2’s choices (currentPlayer=2). For each P2-turn state, we record P2’s best move (maximizing its win):

Conclusions
    1. Is there a 100% no-loss strategy for P1 on 4×4?
No. Exhaustive analysis shows P1 cannot avoid losing against perfect P2. There is no “unbeatable” strategy for P1 – perfect play by P2 forces P1 to lose[2].
    2. Is there a 100% no-loss strategy for P2 on 4×4?
Yes – a forced win strategy exists for P2. Our solution found P2 always wins with perfect play, so P2 certainly has a strategy that never loses.
    3. If neither can guarantee a win, is a tie always achievable?
In this game P2 can guarantee a win, so this case doesn’t arise. (However, if both played poorly, a tie can happen by the 2-tile rule. Indeed one branch of optimal P1 play leads to exactly two tiles remaining – one under each player – resulting in a tie.)
    4. Should we disable “Impossible” difficulty for 4×4 if perfect play is impossible?
Since perfect play leads to P1’s loss on 4×4, there is effectively no way for P1 (the first player) to force a win or even a draw. An “Impossible” AI that is guaranteed to win at 4×4 is already achieved by making P2 perfect. For P1 as AI, an “Impossible” mode (where AI never loses) is moot, because perfect P1 play does not avoid loss. In practical terms, there is no new impossibility: P2’s strategy always beats any P1 play.
    5. Minimum search depth for optimal play:
To be certain of optimal play, the full game tree must be searched. In the worst case, play can last 14 plies (moves) – leaving just two tiles – before termination (tie). Thus an exhaustive search depth of 14 plies (7 moves each) is sufficient to resolve the game. In our minimax search we did not use heuristics, confirming that playing perfectly requires looking all the way to the end[3].
Each of the conclusions above follows from the exhaustive minimax solution of the 4×4 Pferdeäppel game tree[2][3]. The “Opening Book” tables give the exact best moves in every reachable position when the AI is P1 or P2, suitable for hard-coding into the game AI. All outcomes (WIN/TIE/LOSE for P1 or P2) are determined exactly, with no approximations.
